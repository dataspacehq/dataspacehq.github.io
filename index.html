The problem

A renamed column can break a dashboard for three months before anyone notices.

It doesn't take a bad actor — just a routine change nothing was watching. Every team builds their domain a little differently, and nothing enforces the rules; it just documents them. Data mesh promised a fix; in practice it's wikis and tribal knowledge.

  • ✗No two domains look alike — naming, layers, access, PII, all divergent.
  • ✗Your team gates every new dataset, pipeline and grant — or stops trying to.
  • ✗Nobody can safely navigate another team's data, so nobody notices when it breaks.

Questions

The things people ask first.

Isn't this just an API in front of Snowflake?

No. An API is one of several output ports. DataSpaceHQ is a factory for complete, governed data domains — with a lifecycle (ingest → transform → serve), enforced company guardrails, and a fleet console across all of them. The endpoint is the easy part; the governance and consistency are the product.

Does my data leave Snowflake?

Never. DataSpaceHQ is a Native App that runs entirely inside your Snowflake account. Nothing is copied to us or anywhere else. You pay Snowflake for compute directly; we're a software layer on top.

Do we still need to wire SPCS, tasks and RBAC ourselves?

DataSpaceHQ uses them under the hood — you just don't wire them. Ingest is an HTTP endpoint per mart that validates each event against the domain's schema and lands it in the raw layer, the transform is generated for you and runs natively in Snowflake, serving runs on Snowpark Container Services, and governance maps to real Snowflake tags, masking policies and roles. Best practices, by default. If you already run dbt yourself, it keeps working alongside — we're not asking you to move it.

What about data that lives in Databricks or another lakehouse?

On the roadmap: govern and serve data in an external Iceberg catalog (Databricks, Glue) through Snowflake — without moving it. If that's your setup, tell us — we're picking design partners for exactly this.

We already use dbt contracts, or looked at Gable.

dbt contracts only cover models built in dbt. Gable requires instrumenting your producer's codebase. DataSpaceHQ's contracts reach adopted tables too — including data that existed years before DataSpaceHQ did, in a database it didn't build.
